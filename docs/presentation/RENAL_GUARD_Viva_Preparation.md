# RENAL-GUARD — evaluator defense

Version 3.1 · Generated 15 November 2026. Proposed adult Stage 1-to-Stage 2/3 progression within 48 hours, pending supervisor confirmation. No model results exist.

1. **What is the research gap?** A precisely defined progression question with result-availability cutoffs and comparable external labels. Progression prediction already has prior research; we claim no first-of-its-kind novelty.
2. **Why does AKI matter?** The AKI-EPI study found AKI in 57.3% of 1,802 ICU patients during the first week using full KDIGO criteria. This is contextual evidence, not our creatinine-only progression prevalence. Hoste et al., 2015, doi:10.1007/s00134-015-3934-7.
3. **What is the exact target?** Among eligible adults with confirmed creatinine-defined Stage 1 at an ICU index, predict observed creatinine-defined Stage 2/3 strictly after that index through 48 hours. Baseline and follow-up policies need clinical approval.
4. **Is it the same as onset prediction?** No. Onset starts with patients without AKI; progression starts after Stage 1 is established. The course group list describes onset, so confirmation of the revised scope is required.
5. **What does KDIGO Stage 1 mean?** Creatinine 1.5–1.9 times eligible baseline or an increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL, applying the AKI temporal definition. A single elevated measurement without a valid reference need not establish Stage 1.
6. **What defines Stage 3?** Creatinine at least three times baseline or a rise to at least 4.0 mg/dL with AKI established. Full KDIGO also includes kidney replacement therapy and a pediatric eGFR criterion; our proposed adult primary target is creatinine-only.
7. **How will baseline creatinine be chosen?** After auditing available historical measurements, with a reviewed lookback/reference policy and sensitivity analyses. It must not be a future all-stay minimum.
8. **Why 48 hours?** It is a prespecified near-term research horizon; feasibility depends on adequate follow-up. It is not proven to be the optimal clinical interval or the same thing as KDIGO's diagnostic lookback.
9. **What counts as available information?** A result must be known by the index, not merely collected before it. Audit charttime/storetime in MIMIC and the meaning of eICU offsets; revised-result time does not establish original availability.
10. **What if a patient dies, leaves, or has no repeat measurement?** Apply a prespecified competing-event and ascertainment protocol. The outcome can remain unknown. An unobserved progression event is not automatically a negative.
11. **How will you prevent leakage?** Split by patient; keep repeated admissions together; fit preprocessing, imputation and calibration on permitted development partitions; never use future observations or test results for selection.
12. **How will you handle missingness and imbalance?** Audit missingness, include justified indicators, fit imputation on training only, report prevalence and AUPRC, and assess training-only weighting if warranted. No test-set resampling or unreviewed synthetic oversampling is planned.
13. **Why compare Logistic Regression and XGBoost?** Logistic Regression is a transparent baseline; XGBoost tests whether nonlinear interactions add value. LightGBM is optional. A simpler model can be preferred if nonlinear gains are absent or uncertain.
14. **How are hypotheses falsifiable?** Compare predictive discrimination/calibration to an intercept-only prevalence baseline and assess uncertainty. Test nonlinear improvement over LR using a locked, prespecified evaluation. A null or negative finding is valid; exact selection rules are frozen before test access.
15. **Why AUROC, AUPRC and Brier?** AUROC measures ranking across thresholds; AUPRC captures precision-recall tradeoffs and depends on prevalence. Brier measures squared probability error. Calibration compares predicted risk with observed frequencies; accuracy alone is insufficient.
16. **What makes eICU an external test?** Its patients do not enter model fitting, tuning, calibration or threshold selection. The pipeline is frozen first. Similar labels, units and timing are prerequisites; any target recalibration needs separate adaptation/test cohorts.
17. **Can the project fit available resources?** Tabular models can run on CPU, but extraction and repeated large-file scans may be substantial. Source-only synthetic development can proceed while authorized private compute, storage and access are arranged. Current resource adequacy is unverified.
18. **What is complete and who did it?** The release integrates one progression API and React explorer, backend-only staging, corrected-result replay, audit tools and tests: 42 Python, 7 React and 2 real browser tests pass. No real cohort, trained model or evaluation exists. The user has explicitly confirmed nine proposal/design contributions, recorded fairly in the contribution ledger; this is not a claim of sole code authorship or completed experiments.
19. **What would stop external validation?** Incompatible baselines, inadequate follow-up, uncertain availability times, insufficient events or access failure. Report the limitation or revise the protocol with supervision instead of manufacturing comparable labels.
20. **Does a good retrospective score prove patient benefit?** No. Association is not causation, and retrospective performance does not establish clinical utility, safety or benefit. Prospective and workflow evaluation would be separate future work.
